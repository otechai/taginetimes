## The formula

**Research → Fact → Contradiction → Absurdity → Punchline.** Not rumor, not anger, not insult. Every article starts from something we can point to: a law, a statistic, a published audit, a quote that actually exists.

## Sourcing standards

- **If a statistic is used, it has a source** — the High Commission for Planning (HCP), Bank Al-Maghrib, the Cour des Comptes, the CESE, the CSEFRS, or a comparable public body.
- **If a law is discussed, the law exists** — cited by its real name (*Loi-cadre*, *Dahir*, *Bulletin Officiel* reference) wherever practical.
- **If a quotation is used, it is real.** We do not invent quotes and attribute them to real people.
- **If it can't be verified, it doesn't run** — not even if it would make a better joke.

## What we will not do

- Invent private scandals, secret accounts, or unproven criminal allegations against named individuals.
- Report a rumor as if it were a documented fact.
- Attack a private reputation where a documented institutional outcome makes the same point.

See the full [Satire Disclaimer](/disclaimer/) for how this plays out in a given article.

## Corrections

We're a small, mostly anonymous editorial collective — see [About](/about/) — not a newsroom with a corrections desk, but the standard still applies: if an article gets a fact, a figure, or a citation wrong, we fix it. Flag it through the contact details on the About page and we'll take a look.

## Why anonymous, and why that doesn't lower the bar

Bylines are withheld for safety reasons that should be obvious to anyone who has read Moroccan press coverage lately, not to dodge accountability for what's published. The sourcing rules above apply exactly the same whether or not a name is attached to an article — arguably more, since the absence of a byline means the only thing standing behind a piece is whether it's actually true.
